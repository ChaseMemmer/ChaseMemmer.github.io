About Me

I'm a multidisciplinary R&D hardware engineer. Most of my work has lived somewhere between electrical design, mechanical constraints, test, manufacturing, and the person who eventually has to use or build the thing.

  • What I'm Looking For

    I'm looking for a small team building hardware that still has real unknowns in it. I care less about the exact industry than whether engineers are trusted to own hard problems, learn quickly, and stay close enough to the bench to learn from the first build.


  • How I Work

    I usually start by asking what the system actually needs to do, then push to build before the design feels finished. The first ugly prototype tends to tell me more than another week of planning would have. After that, the work is mostly listening carefully: to the test data, to the technician, and to the parts of the design that are quietly making everyone's life harder.


  • Outside the Lab

    Away from work, I like to earn the downtime. Hiking, house projects, long drives. I do better technical work when the rest of life keeps me moving and hard to bore.

Master's Research

June 2024 – December 2025

For my master's research, I studied EMG-based control for a biomimetic prosthetic arm. EMG is messy in a way that looks simple only before you start measuring it. Electrode placement moves. Sweat changes the signal. The same gesture from the same person can look different a few minutes later. The work was about getting useful intent out of that noise without pretending the body was a clean lab instrument.

ABEX Satellite Project

January 2022 – January 2023

I led a small student team designing the energy management system for the ABEX satellite project. The system included power optimization, energy storage, battery charge/discharge control, DC-DC conversion, safety considerations, and communication with the broader satellite architecture. My role covered hardware design, part selection, budgeting, documentation, and keeping the project moving when the interfaces were still changing. It was one of my first real experiences with the difference between designing a circuit and designing a subsystem.


DEA Project

February 2022 – May 2022

For a semester project, I researched, modeled, and built a modular dielectric elastomer actuator system. The project involved LTSpice modeling, KiCad design, oscillator and current-amplifier stages, voltage multiplication, and a final operating voltage around 1600 V. It was a useful mix of theory, circuit design, high-voltage caution, and the very practical lesson that "simple actuator" does not always mean simple electronics.

SCARA Pick-and-Place Robot

January 2021 – May 2021

I built a small SCARA-style pick-and-place robot with a friend as a learning project. We modified a base design, rebuilt parts in SolidWorks, 3D printed much of the structure, and controlled the system using Python and Arduino. The project involved forward and inverse kinematics, motor control, mechanical packaging, and the usual debugging that comes from asking software, electronics, and plastic parts to agree with each other.


Quincy Compressor Co-Op

May 2019 – May 2020

At Quincy Compressor, I worked in the R&D lab supporting compressor testing, lab procedures, data analysis, calibration, contractor coordination, and compliance work. It was an early introduction to industrial engineering reality: test equipment matters, procedures matter, standards matter, and small measurement problems can become expensive if nobody owns them. I also got my first real taste of engineering work that had to support products, labs, technicians, managers, vendors, and deadlines at the same time.


Robotic Mining Competition

September 2018 – May 2020

NASA Robotic Mining was the project that pulled me deeply into hands-on engineering. I worked on mechanical design for a mining robot built to excavate simulated icy regolith under competition constraints. Over three years, I moved from contributor to technical lead, helped redesign major mechanisms, modeled and simulated large assemblies in SolidWorks, and contributed to a robot that won at the national level. It was also where I learned one of my favorite engineering lessons: a clever design is only useful if it survives contact with dirt, rules, time, tired students, and the repair table.


Space Grant Conference

February 2020

After the University of Alabama's success in NASA Robotic Mining, our team was selected to represent Alabama at the National Space Grant Conference in Washington, D.C. I attended as a technical lead and helped present the project to people from NASA, government, academia, and the broader space-technology community. It was an early lesson that building the thing is only half the job. At some point, you also have to explain why it matters.
